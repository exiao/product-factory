# Delegate work

Make delegation produce a useful outcome that can be checked. Reuse the accepted task, context, constraints, and finish line. The task owner remains responsible for integrating and verifying the result.

## Choose how to delegate

Distinguish the work's role:

- Thinking partner: bounded analysis, competing explanations, or critique. Its conclusion is advice, not a verified fact or approval.
- Task executor: a concrete artifact or change with an observable completion check.
- Proactive coworker: a requested monitor or scheduled task with a trigger, meaningful-change rule, and stop condition.

Keep trivial or tightly coupled critical work with the task owner. Delegate independent work when fresh context, parallel coverage, or a suitable model materially helps. Choose from models actually available in the runtime and the user's preferences; do not hardcode a provider or assume a cheaper model produces a cheaper accepted result.

Use supported subagent capabilities for subtasks. Do not create user-owned chats or message another existing task unless the user explicitly authorizes that action. If workers are unavailable, complete feasible work directly and disclose that it was not independently delegated.

## Give the worker a bounded assignment

Include the concrete deliverable, relevant raw evidence and source paths, accepted decisions, edit boundaries, permissions, completion check, and when to stop or escalate. Share enough context to avoid rediscovery without dumping unrelated history. For independent critique, withhold the desired verdict and the builder's self-justification; retain the actual requirements and evidence.

Separate writers by checkout or disjoint task-owned files. Coordinate shared browsers, ports, databases, and deployment targets explicitly. A worktree does not isolate those resources. Preserve existing changes and do not widen external-action permission through a worker prompt.

Continue separate useful work after dispatch. Inspect provider or delivery failures rather than repeatedly launching workers through a broken route. Redirect or stop a worker that has left its assigned scope; preserve and inspect partial work before retrying.

## Integrate and check

Require changed paths or artifacts, relevant commands and observed results, decisions, and unresolved limits. Inspect the actual output and reproduce disputed findings before integrating it. A worker's completion prose, green isolated test, or confidence score is not proof of an integrated result.

For agreed implementation, reuse [make-it-work](../make-it-work/SKILL.md) and its applicable verification. For an unresolved product direction, retain human or explicitly delegated judgment through [product-factory](../product-factory/SKILL.md). Do not convert a critique into an unauthorized edit.

Keep assignment status and handoff evidence in the existing task record. Report what was delivered and verified, what remains pending, and any decision needed. Avoid another dashboard or task system solely to track delegation.

For an explicitly requested recurring coworker, use the runtime's supported scheduling tool. Resolve consequential trigger, destination, resource, cadence, and stop-condition gaps before creation. Save the user's notification intent and stay quiet on unchanged states unless periodic reports were requested. Preparing a prompt is not an active schedule.
