# Confirm understanding

Help the user catch a wrong interpretation before it becomes a plan or product. Reuse the request, project context, and decisions already made.

## Clarify the current task

Summarize the intended outcome, problem, and approach in two or three sentences. Make the audience, boundaries, and success criteria clear where they matter. Separate supported requirements from assumptions; a proposed implementation is not automatically a requirement.

Ask about gaps that could materially change the result, scope, criteria, or authorization. Batch related questions with the available question tool or ordinary chat. Continue independent authorized work while a required answer is pending. A clear task needs no mandatory interview or repeated approval.

Give your own assessment and explain disagreements with evidence. When corrected, check the interpretation and revise affected decisions; do not invent a reason to agree. Keep uncertainty visible when the evidence is incomplete.

Use a sketch, table, diagram, chart, or interactive example when seeing it would make an assumption or choice easier to judge. For example, show rough concept sketches when comparing product directions. Match the format to the question and use a short answer when that is sufficient.

Update the existing brief when writing is authorized. For a clarification-only request, stop with the shared understanding and remaining questions. Otherwise, continue the already authorized task once consequential gaps are resolved. Clarification does not itself authorize implementation or verify the result.
