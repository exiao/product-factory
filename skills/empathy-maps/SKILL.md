# Empathy Maps

Show what is known about a person, what remains uncertain, and which problems need attention. Preserve the user's audience and scope. For a review-only request, report findings without changing the artifact. A map alone cannot establish demand or prove a solution works.

## Frame and gather evidence

Identify the person or user type, their task context, and the question the map should help answer. Reuse the user's framing and available research. State whether the map describes an individual or synthesizes a group. Keep materially different needs and contexts separate. Identify situational constraints that materially affect the task, such as interruptions, unfamiliarity, poor connectivity, or one-handed use, only where relevant. Attach evidence or an explicit hypothesis to each; do not infer constraints from identity or turn examples into a universal checklist.

Use available interviews, observations, support records, reviews, or desk research. When evidence is thin, actively look for relevant published interviews, first-person accounts and community discussions before leaving the map unpopulated. Respect research scope and no-browsing instructions. Read the source, not just a search snippet. We do not need to conduct an interview ourselves to use a person's published statements: attribute them to the speaker and retain their context. Expert guidance and vendor claims can suggest questions but cannot establish a person's thoughts or feelings.

Extract specific statements, reported actions, beliefs and emotions. Separate those from interpretations. If a map combines several people's accounts, name it as a synthesis and preserve attribution on each note; do not put their words into a fictional persona's mouth. Explain material differences between the source population and the intended users. Published accounts support claims about those speakers, not prevalence across a segment.

When synthetic exploration is requested or useful within the task, use [$synthetic-userstudies](../synthetic-userstudies/SKILL.md) to explore remaining questions. Ground the participant context in available research, allow counterexamples, and retain session references. Synthetic statements can populate a map when visibly labeled **SYNTHETIC** at the note level; never relabel them as reported customer evidence. Do not run simulations merely to fill every quadrant. Leave a gap when relevant evidence or a useful labeled hypothesis is unavailable, and state what was checked.

For synthesis from multiple sessions or researchers, first extract notes independently and preserve each person's episode before clustering across people. Start with explicit statements and actions, then add labeled interpretations of thoughts or feelings. Retain participant and source IDs when moving notes; multiple observers or duplicated notes about one episode are not independent corroboration. Independent extraction is useful when group consensus could obscure disagreement, not a required workshop for a small map.

## Build four quadrants

Default to a simple four-quadrant visual labeled with the person and context. The quadrants have no time order. Respect a requested text-only format.

| Quadrant | What belongs here |
| --- | --- |
| **Says** | Attributed statements from direct or published accounts, with exact quotes distinguished from paraphrases. In a synthetic map, label simulated dialogue **SYNTHETIC** and link its session. |
| **Thinks** | Beliefs, expectations, motivations, concerns, or questions. Distinguish what the person reported from your interpretation. |
| **Does** | Concrete actions and workarounds. Distinguish observed behavior from self-reports and hypothetical behavior. |
| **Feels** | Emotions in a particular context, with the trigger when known. Distinguish reported feelings from inferred emotions. |

Keep notes short and specific. Mark each as **observed**, **reported**, **interpretation**, or **SYNTHETIC**, as appropriate. Label other unsupported proposals as hypotheses. Link observations and reports to sources; explain the basis of interpretations. A source must support the attached claim. Put detailed references in a source key so labels do not overwhelm the map. For material claims, preserve the episode when available: trigger, attempted action, current workaround, and consequence. Distinguish a report of a particular event from a general opinion; when the source lacks this context, mark the gap rather than inventing a representative story. Do not rely on color alone for evidence status. No note count is required.

Some notes fit more than one quadrant; place them where they help the reader. Preserve tensions, such as wanting simplicity while repeatedly comparing options, without assuming the behavior contradicts the stated need. When statements and actions appear to differ, preserve both and consider competing explanations such as different situations, incentives, constraints, or changes over time. For a consequential interpretation, name a plausible alternative explanation and the observation that would distinguish them. Avoid stereotypes and unsupported psychological diagnoses. Repeated mentions alone do not establish prevalence.

## Interpret and hand off

Explain the needs, workarounds, and unanswered questions that matter to the task. Describe each problem through the person's goal, the difficulty they face, and its consequence. Separate feature requests from the problems they might address. Identify which uncertain claims could change the conclusion and how to check them. Match the check to the claim: use observation for task behavior, interviews or diaries for context and meaning, appropriately defined analytics for frequency, and a real demand test for payment or adoption.

Deliver the map, source key, and a short note on what it suggests or leaves unanswered. When criteria or walkthroughs are in scope, carry consequential situational constraints and their evidence labels into the test conditions; proposing a condition does not make it an approved requirement. For visual output, render and inspect it at its intended reading size: check legibility, clipping, and source associations. If rendering or inspection is unavailable, state that limit. Keep the format proportionate to the request; no particular style, hosting, or separate approval process is required.

When the task includes illustrated solution scenarios, use [$storyboards](../storyboards/SKILL.md) to show actions and consequences over time. Use [$journey-mapping](../journey-mapping/SKILL.md) for stages, touchpoints, handoffs, or recovery across time. Do not add either artifact just to complete an empathy-map request.
