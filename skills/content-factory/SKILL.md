# Content Factory

Turn real source material and audience judgment into finished content that can be published or tested. Preserve the user's product, voice, scope, settled decisions, and existing authorization. This skill coordinates production; it does not build a content platform or authorize extra publishing, scheduling, spending, or messages.

## Choose the scope

Operations are conversational, not shell commands. Infer them from ordinary requests.

- **Focused request:** do only the requested operation on the named material. A critique reports findings; a revision changes the bounded artifact. Do not initiate a campaign, research project, or redesign as a side effect.
- **Delegated batch or campaign:** use [batch workflow](references/batch-workflow.md). Reuse the audience, objective, sources, formats, quantity, and finish line already agreed. Continue through production and verification within authorization; a script or moodboard does not complete a request for finished media.
- **Settled execution:** route directly to the relevant specialist. An approved script needing an edit does not need new positioning or concepts.
- **Advice or bare invocation:** recommend up to three context-relevant operations and explain the leading choice. Do not execute an advice request. If context already supplies an actionable assignment, proceed with it.

## One useful message, finished output

Define the audience situation → useful insight or demonstrated benefit → supporting evidence → intended response. For educational or entertainment content, the payoff can stand alone; do not force a sales CTA into every piece. Paid creative must connect the promise to the actual product and destination.

Cut unsupported claims, competing messages, ornamental production, and near-duplicate variants before adding output. Preserve a distinctive idea and meaningful proof when simplifying. Volume and polish cannot rescue an empty premise. Demonstrate one representative piece before multiplying an uncertain format; this is a production checkpoint, not a mandatory approval gate. Honor explicit requests for breadth or alternative concepts.

Use existing footage, recordings, product captures, notes, research, and permitted customer material first. Retrieve available sources rather than assigning avoidable collection work to the user. Ask for missing human contribution precisely when it matters. Do not turn missing footage into a fabricated customer story, fake product demonstration, or an undeclared change of deliverable.

## AI content: earn belief and attention

For Eric's AI-led social content and ads, prioritize comedy or education unless he chooses another direction. This is a creative default to test, not a factual claim that other genres cannot work. Use story to create anticipation and payoff; photorealism alone does not make an invented experience authentic. Preserve real testimony and founder experience when belief depends on an event actually happening. Clearly fictional characters and worlds can still carry earned emotion.

Before multiplying concepts, name the intended laugh, useful learning, or other explicitly chosen payoff, why generation helps, and what the viewer is being asked to believe. Read only the guidance needed for the current operation. Preserve the parent assignment through specialist handoffs.

Paid concept development varies audience situations, motivations, proof, or framing before generating executions. Hook iterations remain useful, but distinguish them from new concepts. Organic success informs paid hypotheses; it does not establish conversion performance. Use the relevant platform skill for campaign operations, keeping the user's budget and account boundaries intact.

## Evidence and completion

Keep source facts, user-supplied claims, inferences, illustrative material, and performance observations distinguishable. Trace factual claims to source/version/timeframe where relevant. Never invent testimonials, first-person experiences, statistics, urgency, or product behavior. Generated assets must not masquerade as observed proof. A reference's views do not establish sales performance or permission to reuse its footage.

Use the existing project folder and record; no mandatory dashboard, schema, or new toolchain. At handoffs carry the target asset, audience, message, sources, approved wording, destination, exclusions, and unresolved issue. Preserve links to raw evidence, not just model summaries.

Lead delivery with the actual files or previews and a concise recommendation. Distinguish concept, draft, exported, inspected, published, and measured status without requiring a formal ledger. Inspect rendered assets, not just scripts or successful tool responses. If blocked, deliver the usable work, identify exactly what prevents the agreed finish line, and do not label the batch complete.

Ask only for consequential unresolved choices not already delegated. Publishing or spending approval, when needed, comes after preparing the concrete assets and proposed action. Once authorized, carry it out and verify the destination state; do not repeatedly request the same permission. Stop at the agreed finish line. A weekly plan alone does not create a recurring automation.
