# AI creative judgment

Use when choosing, writing, directing, or reviewing AI-led content. This is a compact creative lens within the existing assignment, not another process or approval gate.

## Choose a payoff worth making

Eric's default is comedy or education for AI-led social content and ads. Treat that as a starting preference, not empirical proof that other genres fail. When direction is open, lead with comedy and education; explain a concrete audience or brief-based reason before recommending a different lane. Follow an explicitly chosen narrative, artistic, or emotional direction. AI assistance with editing an authentic story does not make the underlying experience invented.

- **Comedy:** name the recognizable situation, comic rule or contradiction, escalation, and payoff. Generation should make the exaggeration or impossible situation useful to the joke. Random weirdness and a list of outrageous images do not automatically form a comic premise. Let reactions and pauses do work.
- **Education:** name the question, show a concrete worked example or mechanism, and resolve it with something the viewer can understand or use. Generated imagery can explain hidden processes or visualize an analogy, but illustrative detail is not evidence of an actual event or scientific result.
- **Other selected stories:** establish a want, obstacle, choice, and consequence. Specific characters, performances, world details, and consistent cause and effect can earn emotion in acknowledged fiction. Do not turn the comedy/education preference into a refusal or force jokes into drama.

Before scaling, make the smallest representative execution that contains its payoff: a read-through, rough cut, worked explanation, or complete scene beat. Ask what remains compelling if the audience already knows it is AI-generated. If the answer is only novelty, improve the premise before polishing or multiplying it. This is judgment, not proof of future reach.

## User-selected example: a social observation made visible

Source: [slopject, iPhone Duo Performative Mode](https://x.com/slopject/status/2097849527726190649), posted 2026-09-10 UTC; both attached still images and the post caption inspected directly. Eric explicitly selected this as great AI-generated content. This is a taste reference, not evidence of sales performance.

The first image shows a subway rider with glasses, wired earbuds, a green drink, and a folding phone. The second isolates the phone: its outward-facing panel displays a Joan Didion book cover. The short caption names a fictional feature. Editorial interpretation: the joke turns the desire to appear literary into a literal device function. The everyday setting and specific props make the social observation recognizable; the close-up makes the mechanism legible. The caption lets the viewer complete the joke without explaining it.

Transfer the method, not the exact props or gag: identify a recognizable behavior or status signal, invent one precise feature that exposes its contradiction, and depict it plainly enough for the audience to infer the punchline. A still image, two-panel reveal, or short line may be the complete payoff. Do not inflate a compact visual joke into a mandatory character arc, video, or educational lesson. Photorealism can serve fictional satire without supplying evidence that the device or event exists; preserve clear parody framing where ambiguity matters.

## Know what the viewer is being asked to believe

Separate an authentic captured event, AI assistance with genuine material, a transparent illustration, and fictional performance. A made-up person can tell a fictional story; a made-up customer cannot substantiate a product claim. A fiction label does not support an otherwise unsupported income, health, or performance promise; remove or substantiate the promise itself. Preserve real founder voice, testimony, and demonstrations when the value depends on who actually experienced something.

Use fiction and stylization legibly. Mock interviews or simulated behind-the-scenes scenes belong to the fictional world; do not present them as documentary evidence of a real cast, endorsement, customer, or shoot. Emotional credibility comes from specific choices and consequences, not claims that a generated person suffered or succeeded in real life. Label materially ambiguous depictions appropriately and follow applicable destination requirements.

## Make ads belong to the product

Tie the attention device to an actual audience tension and product contribution. A possible narrative path is surprising event → reaction → question → product-related reveal → consequence → next action. Adapt it to the premise; not every ad needs a character, antagonist, rapid montage, or delayed reveal.

Check whether substituting an unrelated logo leaves the story unchanged. If so, strengthen the mechanism, demonstration, or ending before creating more variations. Keep real product evidence separate from comic exaggeration. Provocation, comments, reported views, press coverage, and awards do not establish incremental sales, profitable acquisition, or audience trust.

## Direct and edit deliberately

Develop a few anchor images and references that express a chosen visual idea. Add only the character, prop, location, and state detail needed for continuity and meaning. A recurring world can accumulate useful assets over episodes; a single ad does not need a world bible. Test frames in motion and compare coverage in the timeline before broad generation.

Review acting, pauses, reactions, spatial continuity, and the sequence's payoff. Bring clips into an edit early. Sound perspective, consistent voices, room tone, music, and motivated effects help separately generated material form one scene. Real performances or hybrid production may help when nuance is central; select within the actual resources and brief rather than declaring one method universally superior.

Use tutorial tools and techniques as dated options. Small frame groups may help spatial consistency but sacrifice detail; individual shots may offer control but cost more. Test upscaling and texture rather than prescribing them: the archive itself contains conflicting preferences. Current tool availability, licensing, generation limits, costs, and model behavior need verification when used. Retain installed tool defaults and the user's explicit provider choices.
