# Customer Research Source Guides

Use these guides to locate public evidence. Adapt the source to the target ICP
and report what could not be accessed. A public source is evidence of the
author's experience or claim, not automatically evidence of the whole market.

## Reddit and forums

Find where the ICP discusses the problem, not only where a product is named.
Useful searches include:

```text
site:reddit.com/r/[subreddit] "[problem or workflow]"
site:reddit.com "[problem]" (recommend OR suggestion OR alternative)
site:reddit.com "[competitor]" (switched OR frustrated OR alternative)
site:news.ycombinator.com "[category or competitor]"
```

Look for tool recommendations, workaround descriptions, competitor complaints,
evaluation questions, and comments that explain why someone switched. Capture
the post and comment URLs, dates, scores or engagement when visible, and the
context around each quote. Engagement helps rank what to inspect; it does not
measure how common the problem is. If a thread tree is incomplete, say so and
do not present the visible subset as the full discussion.

## G2, Capterra, and other reviews

Read multiple rating bands. Three-star reviews often contain balanced unmet
needs; one-star reviews expose failure modes; four-star competitor reviews can
contain specific complaints inside otherwise positive accounts; five-star
reviews reveal valued outcomes and vocabulary. For each review capture the
job, praise, complaint, switching context, unmet need, reviewer segment clues,
rating, date, and direct URL. Do not infer review volume or customer share from
the page without a verified metric.

## Product Hunt and maker communities

Discussion questions reveal pre-purchase objections and comments reveal early
reactions. Founder and builder communities can expose stack choices and
workarounds, but their audience may differ from mainstream buyers. Preserve
the thread context and separate first-hand use from speculation.

## LinkedIn and job postings

Search for practitioners describing an old-to-new workflow, tool evaluation,
or a missed outcome. Job postings can reveal required tools, responsibilities,
and trigger conditions. Treat employer-authored requirements as company claims;
do not infer that every practitioner shares them. Record the post or listing
URL, date, and exact wording.

## YouTube, app stores, and social comments

Tutorial, comparison, and competitor-demo comments can reveal edge cases,
confusion, and active evaluation. For consumer products, inspect one-to-three
star app reviews and relevant community comments, while retaining rating and
version/date where available. Search X or other public social surfaces for
specific complaints, recommendations, and switching language. Public comments
are often skewed toward unusually engaged people; state that limitation.

## Source review checklist

For every source, ask: Is this first-hand experience or a claim about others?
What prompted it? What is directly observed? What is interpretation? Does
another source contradict it? Can a reader open the source and find the quote?
If the answer is no, downgrade the claim to an open question or report the
access gap rather than filling it with a guess.
