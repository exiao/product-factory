# Writer

The user's current instructions take precedence over these voice defaults. Apply them to the requested writing only; they do not authorize publishing or expand the scope of an edit.

Use this skill for articles, blog posts, X or other social posts, newsletters, landing-page copy, ads, product copy, and other externally shared writing attributed to the user.

Do not use it for casual conversation, internal notes, runbooks, plans, PR bodies, error messages, agent prompts, or technical documentation. Those need clear functional prose, not a performed authorial voice.

## Before writing

If the audience and publishing surface are not already clear, ask one question: who is this for, and where will it be published? If the reader's desired reaction or action is also unclear, ask for that in the same question. Do not stack a questionnaire.

When writing about a real page, product, event, company, or artifact, inspect the source first. Use its actual names, numbers, dates, and links.

Load the on-demand references when the task needs worked examples or a deeper revision pass:

- [author patterns](references/author-patterns.md) for guidance on using the current author's supplied samples. These are writing examples, not verified biographical facts; do not turn them into claims about the author.
- [revision prescriptions](references/revision-prescriptions.md) for a compact diagnosis-to-fix map, prosody checks, and show-don't-tell moves.
- [product copy](references/product-copy.md) for conversion surfaces. Reverify live product facts, entitlements, limits, prices, and compliance language each time you use it.

## Non-negotiables

- Never invent a fact, source, number, date, name, result, or experience. Ask, write around the gap, or mark `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.
- Never invent a personality. Preserve the author's point of view, level of certainty, use of "I", and emotional temperature. "Make it human" means remove machine-shaped language, not add a narrator.
- Show the work: use concrete moments, observed outputs, mechanisms, screenshots, or real examples when available. Prefer a specific claim to vague praise.
- Keep claims proportional to evidence. State limits and mistakes plainly.
- Summarize sources in fresh language organized around the piece's argument. Mark borrowed wording as a quotation and attribute it with a link. A citation alone does not mark a quotation.

## Voice and shape

Write conversationally, like a solo founder-builder speaking to a smart peer: direct, opinionated, visually concrete, and honest about uncertainty. Start with a specific moment, result, or problem when the material supports one. Lead with what happened before explaining why it matters.

Use short paragraphs with varied lengths and sentence rhythms. Let commas and longer sentences breathe; do not turn every thought into a clipped fragment. Prefer active, physical verbs and ordinary words over abstractions and jargon. Define necessary jargon inline. End with a concrete consequence, link, or action when the piece needs a close.

Use headings, lists, and emphasis when they make the piece easier to navigate or compare. Match the publishing surface and the user's preferences. Use bold sparingly, sentence-case headings, straight quotes, and inline links. Use backticks for code or technical terms and `@` prefixes for stock tickers. Use emojis rarely and only when the surface supports them.

Choose the organization for the reader's task: follow events in a narrative, build concepts in an explainer, and foreground the change in an announcement. Do not force extra sections or list items.

## Mannered prose

Mannered prose uses decorative language that obscures a straightforward idea. During revision, restate a suspicious sentence plainly. If it still makes no specific claim or adds nothing, delete it; otherwise keep the clearer version. Prefer literal wording when metaphor adds no meaning; remove flourishes that draw attention to the writing without helping the reader understand.

For example, replace "The launch opened a new chapter for our onboarding" with "The launch changed our onboarding", then specify what changed if the source supports it. If no useful detail is available and the sentence adds nothing, delete it. Replace "This paragraph earns its place" with "This paragraph explains the tradeoff" when that is its purpose.

Split sentences when stacked clauses bury the claim, and start a new paragraph when the point changes. Preserve imagery that explains a mechanism and unusual wording that belongs to the author's voice; this is not a ban on metaphor or long sentences.

Check for interchangeable language: if a sentence could fit an unrelated company's post unchanged, replace it with a supported detail or remove it.

## Kill-phrase pass

Before delivering, remove:

- em dashes (a style preference, not evidence of bad writing);
- "delve", "leverage", "harness", "utilize", "cutting-edge", "game-changer", and similar corporate filler;
- formulaic reversals such as "X isn't Y. It's Z.", "It's not X, it's Y.", and "less X, more Y";
- throat-clearing and fake insider framing such as "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "The part nobody talks about", and "What made it work:";
- sycophancy, apology theater, and praise-openers such as "you're absolutely right", "great question", "sorry", and "I'd be happy to help";
- manufactured drops, interpretive trailers, recap endings, and self-applause such as "That's it", "That's the point", "This is why it matters", or "In conclusion";
- decorative triads, verbless noun lists, repeated restatements, hedges that make claims unfalsifiable, and passive sentences with no useful actor.

When removing em dashes, recast the sentence with periods or commas as needed. Do not mechanically substitute colons, semicolons, or parentheses.

Do not flatten every unusual phrase or long sentence. Keep an odd word, aside, joke, or rhythm when it belongs to the author's voice. Cut only what is machine-shaped, unsupported, or unnecessary.

## Modes

For a new piece, draft from the supplied facts, source material, and intended action. Open with the strongest concrete material and keep each paragraph focused on a distinct point.

For a revision, preserve the author's facts and point of view while tightening structure, specificity, rhythm, and clarity. Rewrite as much as needed, but do not add a claim or personality that was not present.

For short marketing surfaces, name the reader and the one action first, then provide several concise options when selection matters. Keep character limits visible when the user supplies them. Make every option stand on its own and run the kill-phrase pass on each.

If asked to audit whether a draft is slop without rewriting it, identify the observable pattern, quote the relevant line, give the concise fix, and stop. Do not guess whether AI wrote it.
