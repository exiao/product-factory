# Short-form hooks

Use this guide when a script needs a TikTok, Reels, Shorts, or other short-form opening. The first seconds have two related jobs: the image or action earns a pause, and the spoken or on-screen language earns the next few seconds. Write both together.

## Opening fields

Record these fields in the first scene or hook note:

- **First frame/action:** what is visible or changing before the viewer has read anything; it should be legible on a small, muted screen.
- **Spoken hook:** a brief, speakable line that names the audience's situation, creates a question, or makes a specific promise.
- **On-screen line:** optional text that complements the voice instead of merely repeating it. Keep it short enough to read in the opening beat.
- **Open loop and payoff:** what the viewer is invited to wonder, and the concrete answer, proof, or result the script will actually deliver.
- **Next beat:** the immediate confirmation that makes staying feel justified.

## Useful patterns

Choose the pattern that fits the audience and the truthful story; do not force every video into a social hook.

- **Result first:** show the changed state or evidence, then ask how it happened.
- **Specific test:** name an action and a bounded condition, then promise the result of the test.
- **Problem-first call-out:** name a recognizable frustration or decision before introducing a product or solution.
- **Audience or POV:** let a defined viewer recognize their situation immediately.
- **Contradiction or comparison:** put two plausible choices, outcomes, or beliefs in tension and resolve the difference later.

For a cold audience, test a viewer/problem-first opening before a product-first opening when the product is not itself the story. A high-intent demo may need the product in frame immediately. The promise must remain accurate either way.

## Script checks

Read the line aloud. Remove throat-clearing, generic greetings, and explanations that close the question before the next beat. Give each spoken beat one job and a visual reason to continue. If the opening promises a reveal, comparison, or result, identify where it is paid off rather than manufacturing suspense.

When an opening underperforms, hold the body of the piece constant and test a different first frame, spoken line, or pairing before rewriting the whole format. Use comparable baselines and actual retention or downstream outcomes; do not treat a universal view or retention threshold as proof.

After a topic change or natural attention dip, add one relevant rehook: a new question, consequence, proof point, or visual change. It should advance the story rather than add random motion or filler.
