# Structure and retention

Short-form pieces often use: hook, setup, escalating proof or demonstration, payoff, and a specific close. Give each spoken beat a visual reason to continue watching. Rehook after a topic shift with a new question, visual, consequence, or example. Cut on the line, breath, or action when that supports comprehension; do not add random motion to compensate for a weak idea.

Long-form pieces need a clear promise, a map of sections, progressive stakes, concrete examples, and a resolved ending. Audit the first minute, section openings, repeated points, unsupported claims, and the distance between setup and payoff. Narrative pieces use character desire, obstacle, choice, consequence, and change; their pacing comes from scene turns rather than forced social hooks.

Timing is evidence. Keep timecodes continuous, leave room for breaths and transitions, and revise copy when narration cannot fit naturally. Retention tactics should serve understanding and the audience's promise, not become a checklist applied to every format.
