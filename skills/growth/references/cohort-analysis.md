# Cohort analysis

Aggregate funnel rates can hide performance, accessibility, or audience differences. Slice by entry point, acquisition source when available, device and platform, geography where appropriate, role, new versus returning status, and session-one behavior. Use only dimensions that are lawful, relevant, and large enough to interpret responsibly.

For every cohort comparison record:

1. Cohort definition and start date.
2. Eligibility and exclusions.
3. Numerator, denominator, and observation window.
4. Sample size and missingness.
5. Difference from the comparison cohort and uncertainty.
6. Plausible confound and the cheapest check that could distinguish it.

If newer devices retain better, the cause might be audience mix or performance. Instrument step timing and errors before changing targeting or copy. If one acquisition source activates better, check message match, user intent, and sample composition before scaling it.

Use cohort findings to choose a test or diagnostic, not as proof that a segment is inherently better or that a correlated behavior causes retention. Avoid sensitive segmentation unless the user has a legitimate purpose, appropriate governance, and enough evidence to avoid harm.
