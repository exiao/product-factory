# Paywall and retention

## Upgrade experience

Assess what the user has already done, what is blocked, and whether the paid capability is understandable. Test a contextual feature preview, benefit explanation, plan comparison, price presentation, and a clear continue or decline path. Avoid interrupting an essential task or showing repeated prompts after dismissal; set a measured cooldown and track annoyance signals.

Define paywall metrics precisely. For example, impression-to-action is not the same as action-to-completion, and a trial start is not a paid conversion. Follow users through renewal and retention before calling a paywall change successful. Include refund, cancellation, support, and payment-pending outcomes as guardrails where relevant.

## Trial and pricing presentation

Test timing, full versus limited access, reminder cadence, and upgrade path only when the terms remain clear. State the amount, billing period, renewal behavior, eligibility uncertainty, and cancellation route at the point of decision. Discounts, countdowns, urgency, and loss framing require a real basis; never fabricate scarcity or obscure the free path.

## Cancellation

Use an optional short reason survey to learn whether the problem is price, low usage, missing capability, temporary need, technical failure, or something else. A relevant pause, downgrade, help path, or workaround may be offered once, but the user must be able to cancel directly. Preserve access and data according to the stated policy and provide a reactivation path without coercion.

## Payment recovery

Separate voluntary churn from failed payment. For a failed charge, distinguish retryable failures, closed or invalid instruments, and authentication requirements. Communicate pending status as pending, retry on a documented schedule, and stop retrying when the provider says the failure is permanent. Measure recovery rate, support contacts, involuntary churn, and downstream complaints.

## Retention signals

Use behavior such as declining key-action frequency, removed seats, exports, support changes, or billing-page visits as investigation signals. Set thresholds from the product's baseline and validate interventions against a holdout or comparable cohort where feasible. A discount that preserves a subscription but trains cancellation behavior can be a poor long-term result.
