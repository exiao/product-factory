# Referral and affiliate programs

Use for a requested referral/affiliate design or diagnosis. Distinguish customers recommending a product, affiliates selling to an audience for compensation, and inherent product collaboration/sharing. These mechanisms have different incentives, trust, and measurement. Referral planning does not authorize deploying prompts, contacting customers, or issuing rewards.

Establish the current sharing behavior, audience, product value moment, acquisition costs, contribution economics, and available evidence. Choose a trigger after a relevant useful outcome, a natural share mechanism, a clear recipient benefit, an attributable conversion event, and a reward condition. Do not assume a particular share channel or double-sided reward always converts best.

Compare single-sided, double-sided, noncash, and tiered incentives based on user motivation, cost, eligibility, and abuse exposure. State terms clearly: who qualifies, reward value, timing, caps, exclusions, refunds/reversals, and expiration if applicable. Avoid misleading urgency, hidden requirements, contact-list uploads by default, or making normal product access contingent on referrals.

Instrument eligible users → prompt exposure → share attempt → delivered invitation or usable link → recipient visit → signup → qualified activation/payment → reward issued. Define denominators, deduplication, attribution windows, and new-customer eligibility. A share attempt is not a delivered invitation, and a signup is not a paid retained customer.

Diagnose the first supported loss: low awareness, share friction, irrelevant recipient/offer, destination failure, poor activation, attribution gaps, or reward delivery defects. For low participation, first test whether people have received enough value to recommend the product; do not simply add more prompts. Compare referred and nonreferred cohorts with the same eligibility and observation window, while acknowledging selection bias. Do not promise higher LTV from generic referral benchmarks.

Calculate cost per qualified incremental customer using both sides' reward costs plus operational/tooling costs over a stated horizon. Distinguish cash expense, marginal service cost, and foregone revenue. Compare to contribution, not gross revenue. Attributed referrals can include people who would have joined anyway; a feasible experiment or holdout gives stronger evidence of incrementality.

Define controls proportionate to observed risks: duplicate accounts, self-referrals, code leakage, invalid/refunded purchases, bots, and reward farming. Verify eligibility before rewarding, preserve an auditable reward state, and use idempotency or reconciliation to avoid duplicate payouts. Avoid blanket blocks that penalize legitimate shared households without evidence. For affiliate programs, document commission rules, allowed promotion, disclosure requirements, and rights; verify current legal/platform requirements for a live implementation.

Deliver the proposed loop, economics assumptions, event definitions, honest terms, abuse controls, and a bounded test with a primary metric, trust/retention guardrails, and a stop/revise rule. Use growth's experimentation guidance to evaluate results.
