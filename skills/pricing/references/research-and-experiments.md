# Pricing research and experiments

## Van Westendorp

Ask each qualified respondent:

1. At what price is this too expensive to consider?
2. At what price is it so cheap that quality is doubtful?
3. At what price is it becoming expensive but still worth considering?
4. At what price is it a bargain?

Plot cumulative distributions and inspect the marginal-cheapness, marginal-expensiveness, optimal-price, and indifference intersections. Treat the resulting range as a hypothesis. Segment the curves by persona, use case, and geography and pair them with observed purchase behavior.

## MaxDiff, Gabor-Granger, and conjoint

Use MaxDiff to rank candidate features or benefits for packaging. Use Gabor-Granger price variants to estimate a demand curve, and conjoint when customers choose among bundles where feature tradeoffs matter. Randomize presentation, screen for the right buyer, and record purchase intent alongside reasons for rejection. Avoid presenting a single survey statistic as causal evidence.

## Usage-to-value analysis

Instrument usage, outcomes, expansion, retention, support cost, and variable cost. Compare retained and churned cohorts, then identify usage thresholds that predict activation, renewal, or expansion. A value metric should follow those thresholds without charging customers for activity that creates no value.

## Experiment design

Define the decision, eligible segment, baseline, price or package variants, exposure unit, guardrails, and observation window before launch. Track conversion, net revenue, refund rate, activation, retention, support burden, and cost to serve. Check for seasonality, acquisition mix, tax/currency effects, and novelty. Stop or roll back for predefined harm; do not mutate live billing or store catalogs without explicit authorization.

## Price-change communication

For increases, compare grandfathering, delayed transition, added value, and plan restructuring. Explain the reason and effective date, preserve contract obligations, and monitor existing-customer churn separately from new-customer conversion. The right outcome is durable net revenue and customer value, not merely a higher displayed price.
