# Packaging and offer design

## Tier architecture

Two tiers can work when the product has a clear entry and scale distinction. Three tiers often provide entry, recommended, and premium choices. More tiers require a real segment or usage difference; extra columns that do not change a buyer’s decision add cognitive load.

For each tier state:

- intended persona and job to be done
- included capabilities and meaningful limits
- value metric and overage behavior
- support, security, integrations, and administrative access
- upgrade trigger and what remains available after downgrade

Use feature gating when capabilities have distinct value, usage limits when value scales with volume, support or security when buying process changes, and API/customization when the customer’s operating model changes. Keep table-stakes features available where withholding them would prevent evaluation.

## Personas and value metrics

Segment by company size, use case, sophistication, industry, buying process, or outcome. Map each segment’s alternatives, budget owner, success metric, adoption barrier, and expansion path. Choose a value metric only when greater usage predicts greater customer value, is measurable, and cannot be gamed.

Common candidates include seats, usage, records, transactions, feature modules, or a platform fee plus usage. Test whether a customer can predict the bill and whether the metric stays aligned when the product or customer workflow changes.

## Freemium, trial, and paid entry

Freemium fits products with low marginal cost, a clear upgrade boundary, or network/referral value. A trial fits products that need time, setup, or full access to demonstrate value. A reverse trial can expose premium value and then leave a useful free tier. Decide whether payment details are required by measuring trial quality, support burden, refund risk, and conversion rather than copying a benchmark.

Consumer subscriptions may pair a baseline subscription with consumables when extra generations, analyses, scans, or reports deliver discrete repeatable value. Explain what one unit buys and avoid making the subscription feel incomplete. Validate store rules, refunds, and customer comprehension before launch.

## Enterprise

Use custom terms when procurement, security, implementation, support, or contract requirements materially change the offer. Price the additional service and risk explicitly. Enterprise packaging can combine platform fee, seats, usage, onboarding, support, or outcome terms; model worst-case cost to serve before promising a discount.
