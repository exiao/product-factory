# Lenses and synthesis

| Lens | Questions that change a decision |
| --- | --- |
| User Advocate | Who benefits or pays the cost? What happens in actual use, including accessibility and failure? What user evidence is missing? |
| Architect | Which dependencies, interfaces, invariants, and ownership boundaries make this coherent or fragile? |
| Skeptic | Which assumption could invalidate the plan? What contradicts the favored answer? How would it fail? |
| Pragmatist | What is the smallest useful implementation? What can this team operate and maintain? |
| Innovator | Is there a materially different way to meet the same need, including eliminating the work? |
| Temporal Analyst | What changes over time? Which decisions are reversible, which create lock-in, and what should be sequenced first? |

For architecture, emphasize ownership and operational feasibility. For risk, emphasize failure and time. For UX, prioritize user consequences. For strategy, consider alternatives and long-term commitments. Choose by the question, not keyword matching alone.

For each material tension, state both arguments fairly, then classify it: resolvable through a better option, a genuine tradeoff, or dependent on missing evidence. Explain what evidence would resolve the last case. Weight arguments by relevance and support, not by the number of personas repeating them.

Compare the synthesis with the original question to find assumptions nobody tested. Distinguish lack of evidence from disagreement. Order next steps by urgency and information value. A useful final verdict says what to do under the current evidence and what specific finding would change it.
