# Another perspective

Frame the decision, constraints, and evidence. Choose 2–4 council members whose expertise, incentives, lived experience, or time horizons create useful disagreement; include someone who bears the consequences. Honor requested members. Use [these lenses](references/lenses-and-synthesis.md) as starting points, not a fixed cast. Briefly introduce each member and why their perspective matters.

Give each the same evidence without a preferred verdict. Ask: What should we do, why, what does everyone else miss, and what would change your mind? Have members challenge the strongest opposing argument and revise when warranted.

For consequential or contested decisions, use independent subagents when available; otherwise simulate the council and say so. Represent real people's perspectives as hypotheses, never actual testimony or endorsement.

Synthesize by evidence, not votes. Preserve strong dissent, expose shared assumptions, and seek options that resolve disagreements. End with a recommendation, its main tradeoff, and the next check most likely to change it. Keep discussion proportional; omit transcripts unless requested.
