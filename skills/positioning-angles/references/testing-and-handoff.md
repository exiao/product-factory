# Test and hand off positioning

For each serious angle record the intended audience, promise, proof, objection, proposed surface, and decision-changing assumption. If a product has several audiences, map them separately rather than constructing one generic headline for all of them.

Distinguish evidence types. Customer language can establish relevance, observed usage can reveal jobs, conversion experiments can compare promises, and retention can test whether the product fulfills the expectation. Viral anecdotes and before/after view counts are not controlled evidence that wording caused the change or that revenue improved.

A useful test keeps audience, offer, placement, and measurement as comparable as practical. Define the outcome that matters, observation window, cost bounds when relevant, and stopping criteria. Check comprehension as well as conversion: an attractive but misleading promise can create low-quality acquisition and later churn. Treat small or confounded results as preliminary.

Use a short testing log when repeated comparisons warrant it: angle, audience, surface, date, evidence, outcome, uncertainty, and next decision. Do not invent win probabilities or statistical confidence from subjective ratings.

Hand the chosen frame to [$content-strategy](../../content-strategy/SKILL.md) for channels and formats, [$copywriting](../../copywriting/SKILL.md) for the page/ad argument, and [$hooks](../../hooks/SKILL.md) for openings. [$idea-validation](../../idea-validation/SKILL.md) can compare the buyer/pitch/price combination and demand-test plan across ideas. A promise change that requires a new feature remains a separate product decision; flag it instead of silently building it.
