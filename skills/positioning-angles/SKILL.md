# Positioning angles

Decide what the offer means to a specific audience before optimizing the words. Read the supplied product truth, intended buyer, use context, alternatives, and available evidence. Reuse existing decisions and ask only for missing context that materially changes the frame. A narrow angle question needs an angle, rationale, and the test that could disprove it; a full positioning brief warrants a broader comparison.

Identify the customer's job or buying moment and the actual alternative, including a manual workaround or doing nothing. Generate distinct frames based on different jobs, situations, outcomes, or credible mechanisms rather than paraphrasing one headline. For an open promise-options request, five short lines per idea is a useful starting point; honor another requested count and cover every named idea.

Each proposed promise must be supported by the product or clearly labeled as a future hypothesis. Do not invent results, testimonials, capabilities, credentials, or urgency to make an angle persuasive. Recommend a first test and name the assumption that could change the recommendation. Use [$writer](../writer/SKILL.md) for Eric-authored voice, [$hooks](../hooks/SKILL.md) for opening-line execution, and [$copywriting](../copywriting/SKILL.md) for message structure when that work is requested.

Use the applicable lenses in [references/positioning-lenses.md](references/positioning-lenses.md): behavior substitution, consequence and urgency, substance without adjectives, reason-now, audience/region, and content or personal-brand positioning. They are diagnostic tools, not mandatory gates. Entertainment, convenience, and evergreen products can be valuable without a crisis or macro trend.

Separate the durable brand promise from the acquisition promise and the reason customers stay. Inspect incoming audience intent, available interviews, queries, action choices, and feature engagement before assuming the most differentiated feature is the right headline. Current users and measured clickers may not represent prospective customers; account for sampling, exposure, and funnel stage. Use available authorized analytics rather than assuming an account, tool, or project-specific query exists.

A positioning brief should give the audience and situation, current alternative, central promise, mechanism/proof, meaningful difference, and main objection. Add the chosen first test and rejected alternatives only where they clarify the decision. Keep copy hypotheses separate from measured winners. Read [references/testing-and-handoff.md](references/testing-and-handoff.md) for evidence and downstream use.

Positioning work does not authorize a product redesign, ad launch, publishing, customer outreach, analytics configuration change, or paid experiment. Continue such work when included in existing user authorization; do not require a new approval for routine steps already in scope.
