# Quick Brainstorm

Reuse the user's problem, constraints, and settled choices. Generate five distinct options by default, or follow the requested count. Keep ordinary wording or creative variation requests lightweight.

For product or service ideas:

- Anchor options to one concrete person, starting situation, obstacle, and desired result. Preserve the same scenario so differences are meaningful.
- Vary how help works: who does the work, what input is needed, how decisions are made, what feedback changes the next result, or what constraint is removed. Feature categories, renamed assistants, and layout variants are not distinct mechanisms.
- For each option, give a short worked example from input through intervention to changed result. Explain why it could beat the current workaround, what burden it introduces, and how it handles the hardest tension in the brief.
- Challenge the strongest options: identify the likely failure, the assumption that would reverse the recommendation, and the smallest discriminating test. When output quality is the uncertainty, test an actual sample or manual delivery before UI polish.
- Recommend the most promising experiment, without declaring an untested winner. Discard weak options rather than endorsing all five. If none is convincing, identify the missing insight and propose a different mechanism or targeted inquiry; do not use more screens to conceal the gap.

Include estimated probabilities only when requested or useful for a defined uncertain event. State what the estimate refers to and label unsupported estimates as subjective; idea-generation likelihood and probability of product success are different quantities. Never treat invented percentages or simulated enthusiasm as demand evidence.

Finish with the assumptions that would materially change the answer. Keep the response proportionate; a compact comparison is usually enough.
