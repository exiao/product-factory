# Temporal scenarios and archive comparisons

## Experiences spanning time

Use when delay, repeated contact, changing needs or operational handoffs affect the task. Track a compact event record: elapsed time, external event, what the persona knows, attempted action, response, pending dependency and next trigger. Reuse the persona's stable ID and relevant state; do not invent events merely to complete a timeline.

Label events and time jumps as supplied, observed or simulated. A narrated week is not a week of observed use. Preserve no-response, incomplete, abandonment and recovery states. If comparing paths, keep exposure and opportunity to reach each state visible; complaint counts among those who reached a screen do not measure conversion. Use real diary or longitudinal evidence to investigate behavior over time.

## Evaluating simulations against archived research

Use only when evaluating the simulator is requested or materially needed. Confirm that original stimuli and task instructions are recoverable; a findings deck alone cannot reconstruct an interface test.

Before the run, separate legitimate pre-study context from held-back outcomes. Define the population, tasks, comparison rule and scope of any accuracy claim. Freeze predictions before revealing findings. Compare documented matches, contradictions, missed findings and predictions the real study did not test; unmentioned predictions are not automatically false positives. Use distinct studies for development and evaluation, and disclose prior exposure or likely contamination. Do not tune against the same findings and then call them a holdout.

Archive comparisons remain limited by the original sample, methods, stimulus fidelity, missing records and possible model exposure. Agreement is case-specific evidence, not general proof of human fidelity. Preserve all attempts; do not select favorable runs.
