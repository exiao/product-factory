# Simulation quality review

Use this compact review before synthesizing a consequential synthetic study. It is a qualitative check on the session and does not require another model or a scored evaluation pipeline for every interview. Preserve the original attempt and its evidence if a rerun is useful; do not replace an invalid attempt with a clean-looking result.

## Session quality

Check the transcript, task record, and any supplied or captured interface state for:

- **Leading questions:** the researcher presupposed a problem, preferred answer, frustration, urgency, or product benefit.
- **Solution leakage:** the preferred solution, conclusion, or another persona's answer entered the participant context before the relevant concept reaction.
- **Persona drift:** goals, constraints, experience, alternatives, or access needs changed without an explicit context change.
- **Invented product knowledge:** the participant reacted to capabilities, policies, or outcomes that were not supplied or observed.
- **Incomplete or evasive responses:** a missing answer was treated as agreement, a refusal as a positive signal, or an unresolved turn was omitted from synthesis.

Flag the affected turns and explain how the issue limits the conclusion. A valid session may contain indifference, uncertainty, satisfaction with an existing alternative, disagreement, or no finding. Do not force a complaint or rerun until a preferred result appears.

## Calibration and accuracy claims

Treat claims about simulator accuracy, benchmark size, or agreement with real users as claims requiring supporting evaluation. Where calibration data exists, state its population, task, metric, comparison method, and limits. Agreement between models is not evidence of agreement with customers. Synthetic findings remain hypotheses unless supported by appropriate real-user or product evidence.

## Accessibility limits

Role-play can suggest accessibility concerns and questions for inspection. A fictional disability or access profile cannot establish lived experience or prove that an interface is accessible or inaccessible. Verify relevant barriers with the actual interface, suitable assistive technology or inspection, and real participant evidence when needed. Do not present a simulated perspective as lived testimony.
