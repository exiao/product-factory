# Phase-aware question bank

Select and adapt questions to the conversation. These are examples, not a mandatory script. Ask one question at a time and avoid assuming a problem occurred. All responses in this workflow remain synthetic, including apparently specific past episodes.

## Problem discovery: circumstances and episodes

- What were you trying to accomplish that day?
- Walk me through the last time you did [activity].
- What happened next?
- What did you use to do that?
- Who else was involved?
- What other options did you consider?
- What made you choose that approach?
- What, if anything, got in the way?
- What happened as a result?
- What were you weighing at that point?
- How did you know you were finished?

## Alternatives, exceptions, and tradeoffs

- Tell me about a time your current approach worked well.
- What was different on that occasion?
- When did you decide this was not worth pursuing?
- What took priority instead?
- What did you do when you could not continue?
- What did you give up to make that work?
- What led you to change your previous approach?

## Attitudes and meaning

Use attitudes to explore interpretation, not as proof of behavior. Tie them to an episode where possible.

- How did you feel at that point?
- What did that outcome mean to you?
- What were you expecting to happen?
- How did that compare with what happened?

## Concept reactions: Right Solutions only

Introduce the exact stimulus after discovery, if requested. These questions explore hypothetical interpretation and objections; they do not validate adoption or payment.

- What do you think this offers?
- What do you expect to happen after this action?
- What is unclear here?
- How does this compare with your current approach?
- What would you need to know to assess the terms?
- What could make this unsuitable for your situation?

## Walkthrough: Right Way

Keep observed state separate from simulated interpretation.

- What are you trying to accomplish at this step?
- What action appears available?
- What do you expect that action to do?
- What response appeared after the action?
- What could you do next or if it failed?

## Wrap-up

- What have I not asked that matters here?
- Which assumption should we investigate with real users next?

For an autonomous interview, begin with a recent episode and follow its most consequential uncertainty. Include an exception or alternative rather than forcing a pain narrative. End with hypotheses and next research questions, not a validated problem ranking.
