# Content and motion checks

Evaluate the rendered design against its accepted purpose and direction. A gradient, card, serif, glass surface, or animation is not a defect by itself. When calling a design generic, identify the repeated choice and the harm it causes for this product. Preserve purposeful atmosphere and storytelling when they help the experience.

Check visible claims such as customer counts, ratings, metrics, and screenshots against available product evidence. Give the claim's location and a supported correction when proof is missing. Keep design rationale, implementation notes, and research qualifications in the handoff or a requested review surface rather than the apparent product UI. For a prototype, use one clear disclosure and add local wording only where a person could mistake simulated behavior for a real charge, analysis, or result.

Judge motion at its actual frequency. Frequent actions should respond promptly; a transition that feels pleasant once may delay repeated work. For generated images or motion, inspect the integrated crop, intermediate frames, joins, loading, and reduced-motion or static fallback. Distinguish illustrated behavior from verified demonstration.

When a control moves or disappears, check whether people can still find the task, dismiss temporary UI with a keyboard, and return focus to a visible relevant control. For a temporary proposal, verify that dismissal preserves the original and that acceptance and undo work.
