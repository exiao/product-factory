# Optional production coordination

Use separate assignments when requested or when independent story work or review materially helps a substantial production. Do not create a full pipeline for a single prompt, small trim, or straightforward asset. Role briefs describe responsibilities; they do not create agents, grant tools, or authorize spending, publication, messages, or scheduled monitoring.

## Select the relevant roles

- [Director](roles/director.md): the main task owns treatment, direction, canonical shot list, and resolution of creative notes.
- [Story editor](roles/story-editor.md): a bounded narrative or dialogue revision before production, or a specific story problem found later.
- [Content critic](roles/content-critic.md): independent judgment of a supplied cut against its actual brief.
- [Content QA](roles/content-qa.md): technical verification of that cut against export requirements.

Load only briefs relevant to the current assignment. Follow the user's delegation and model preferences; a role name does not require a particular model. Use subagents within the current task when available and authorized, not new user-owned tasks unless requested. If separate review cannot be provided, label a same-task review as such rather than claim independence.

## Make each assignment concrete

Supply the treatment or relevant excerpt, stable shot IDs, current draft/cut version, accessible asset paths, essential decisions and flexible details, bounded scope, expected output, and file ownership. Include the selected role brief itself or a path the worker can read. Do not assume a worker inherits the right context or can access the main task's filesystem or media tools. Make the requested outcome and output destination explicit, including which files may be edited and what is out of scope. Name the checks that establish completion and any known access or inspection limits. Reuse the existing treatment and production document; fill only gaps relevant to the assignment instead of duplicating every field in a separate handoff template.

Before treating an assignment as ready, confirm the receiving worker can access the required source assets and distinguish essential decisions from permitted variation. Before treating delivery as complete, confirm the output exists at the promised accessible destination and identifies the source/cut version and affected scene or shot IDs. Where a media transfer is involved, compare the receiving copy's identity; use stream/decode checks when file integrity needs verification. These establish delivery and file facts, not creative quality or a claim that the film was watched. Missing inspection capability limits the verdict; it does not prevent delivery of the completed artifact.

Keep the shared production document under one coordinating owner. Scene sections hold authoritative script text; shot records reference it. Assign story edits to the scene sections and direction edits to the shot records, and reconcile timing changes before integration. By default, workers return proposed shot-list changes and write their own assigned artifacts. For delegated editing, assign explicit output paths and preserve source takes and previous cuts. A role brief is not a reason to withhold an edit that the user has requested within that role's scope.

Story work may run alongside independent preparation when dependencies allow. Critic and QA can run in parallel once both have the same complete cut and brief. Do not send them different revisions or pass selected stills as the complete film. Verify the identity of copied media when it crosses hosts; record version/path or hash in the findings.

## Resolve and finish

Ask reviewers for located findings, their consequence, proposed correction, evidence inspected, and limitations. The director resolves creative disagreements against the treatment; observed technical defects remain distinct from taste choices. Use the existing shot-list revision loop to revise affected ranges, update timing and sound dependencies, and check the changed version. Do not repeat unrelated checks without a reason.

Never describe probe output, still frames, or transcripts as full audiovisual inspection. If the needed capability is unavailable, deliver completed work and identify the unverified judgments. Do not fabricate independent approval or stall a requested partial deliverable waiting for publication.

Return the accessible artifact, current production-document version, meaningful changes, and remaining limitations. Stop when the requested production and appropriate checks are complete; these references do not establish an ongoing monitor or automatic release gate.
