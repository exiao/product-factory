# Short-form first frames

Use this guide when directing a short-form cold open or a hook-led product video. Judge the first frame muted and at phone size. The visual should earn a pause; the spoken line or overlay should give that pause a reason to continue.

## Direct the pair

- Start with a visible, diegetic action or meaningful change: a result appears, an object is opened, a comparison is revealed, or a subject enters with intent.
- Let the action imply a question or consequence. Movement alone is decoration, not a hook.
- Start the spoken hook in the same opening beat so the visual and language overlap. They may reinforce each other or create a deliberate contrast, but neither should depend on a later shot to make sense.
- If the product is not the story, begin with the viewer's situation and reveal the product as the response. A high-intent demonstration can show the product immediately when that is what the audience came to inspect.

## Record in the shot plan

For the opening shot, note:

- first-frame composition and physical action;
- the related spoken line or scene-line reference;
- any short overlay and where it will be composited;
- what the next shot confirms or reveals;
- continuity requirements for hands, props, screen state, identity, and lighting.

Keep the frame readable without relying on generated text, logos, UI, or subtitles. Add those in post when needed. Describe camera capture, subject action, environment, light sources, and the beginning-to-end change with concrete verbs rather than generic “viral” or “cinematic” labels.

## Acceptance check

At the opening, a viewer can tell what changed or what deserves attention before reading every word. By the next few seconds, the line and image establish a truthful question or promise, and the planned body contains the evidence or payoff. Reject dead frames, unexplained spectacle, random jump cuts, and visual motion that does not serve the story. If the hook is weak, change one variable at a time: first action, spoken line, overlay, or reveal timing.
