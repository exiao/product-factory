# Video prompting

Open with how the camera captures the shot. Then specify the subject and physical action, environment, real light sources, tactile texture, color, mood, and how the shot evolves from beginning to end. Prefer precise verbs such as dolly push, shoulder-camera drift, whip-pan, rack focus, orbit, or locked-off tripod over “moves around.”

Example:

> Locked-off 35mm camera in a narrow neon-lit ramen shop; a couple eats in slow synchrony, steam rises from the bowls, condensation fogs the window, and a magenta sign flickers across wet glass; shallow focus and warm grain hold the intimate mood as the camera racks focus from the broth to their faces.

Keep a prompt focused. Name aspect ratio and duration in the generation settings when available. Do not rely on “cinematic,” “viral,” or “realistic” alone. Avoid asking a video model for readable text, logos, app UI, or subtitles; composite those after the footage exists.
