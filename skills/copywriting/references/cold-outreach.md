# Cold outreach and follow-ups

Use for requested B2B cold emails, prospect-specific openers, or follow-up drafts. Reuse the recipient's role, public business context, the sender's actual offer, and the desired response. Missing proof is a reason to use a supported mechanism, not invent a result. Use first-customer-finder when the task also needs a qualified prospect list; use writer for Eric-authored voice.

Tie personalization to the problem: an observed workflow, explicit request, or relevant public change should explain why this offer might matter. A job posting or funding event is not proof the recipient has a particular pain. State the observation accurately, then phrase uncertain relevance as a question. Avoid private details, fake familiarity, invented conversations, and implied knowledge of internal metrics.

A useful shape is observation → relevant problem → truthful value/proof → one easy next step. Choose a reply, resource, introduction, or meeting ask appropriate to the relationship and request; no single CTA universally wins. Keep the message concise and readable without a feature dump. Subject lines should honestly describe the topic, never impersonate an internal message or use a false Re:/Fwd: prefix.

Each follow-up should add a relevant fact, demonstration, useful resource, or different supported angle. Do not repeat an unsupported claim or guilt the recipient for silence. Adapt cadence and count to context and applicable sending rules rather than importing benchmark schedules. Respect opt-outs, declines, and stated final-contact boundaries. A promised final message should actually end the sequence unless the recipient re-engages or separately invites contact.

Deliver subject, body, and optional follow-ups, with source notes and unverified assumptions outside the customer-facing copy. Check names, product claims, links, and the intended ask. Do not turn source-backed personalization into a claim of prior personal interaction. Drafting does not authorize sending or enrollment in a sequence; preserve existing sending authorization without expanding recipients or scope.
