# Copywriting

Read supplied product material and `.agents/product-marketing-context.md` when present. Identify the surface, reader, traffic/awareness context, offer, supported proof, and primary action. Reuse known context; ask only for missing information that materially changes the copy. For a supplied URL, inspect the source before inventing details; a complete supplied excerpt can suffice for a narrow rewrite.

This skill owns message structure and conversion logic. Use [$writer](../writer/SKILL.md) for Eric-authored voice and phrasing when applicable, preserving the user's brief first. Use [$hooks](../hooks/SKILL.md) when the task centers on opening lines; do not load an entire writing workflow for a single requested sentence.

Connect the promised outcome to the concrete mechanism. Features support benefits; evidence supports claims. Never fabricate numbers, testimonials, customer experiences, scarcity, credentials, or guarantees. Draft around missing specifics or mark them as needing a source. Preserve necessary qualifications instead of removing them to sound confident.

Match the shape to the surface. A page can use a value proposition, explanatory subhead, accurate CTA, proof, benefits, how it works, objections, and closing action. Choose sections based on the actual decision; do not impose all of them on every page. Match a landing page to its referring ad, help pricing readers choose, and connect feature pages to real use cases.

For product UI simplification, decide whether each text element needs to exist before rewriting it. Remove headings, helper text, and repeated CTAs that merely restate the screen title or an already clear control. Keep the shortest wording that lets the person predict the action and its result; do not use a shorter ambiguous label just to reduce word count. Preserve accessible names, necessary qualifications, error recovery, and information needed for the decision. Place contextual guidance where it becomes useful instead of front-loading the entire workflow.

If the clutter comes from competing controls or premature choices, identify the structural issue and use [$interaction-design](../interaction-design/SKILL.md) within an authorized UX revision. A copy-only request does not authorize changing behavior; flag the issue briefly and complete the requested copy work. Do not compensate for confusing interaction design by adding more explanation. When editing an interface, inspect the copy in its rendered context, including the relevant narrow width and changed states.

For short copy, deliver the requested length directly. A useful event blurb names the reader's tension, what concretely happens, and what they leave with. Email preheaders extend rather than repeat subjects. In-app announcements state what changed, why it matters, and the next action. Verify current platform limits when relevant; historical character budgets are suggestions, not universal constraints.

CTAs must accurately describe the next step and commitment. Do not promise a free trial, download, or instant result unless it exists. Favor readable, specific language over superlatives and generic hype. Keep a professional register for B2B surfaces and preserve the source's real differentiators.

Give alternatives and rationale when choosing direction is part of the request. Otherwise deliver the copy, without an obligatory chooser page or follow-up questionnaire. If an interactive preview is requested, build it; publish only when publishing is authorized. Report hypotheses as hypotheses, without invented win probabilities.

Read [references/formats.md](references/formats.md) for ad-script, short-blurb, and testing patterns. Writing copy does not authorize posting, sending email, or launching campaigns.

For B2B cold emails, prospect-specific personalization, and follow-up drafts, read [cold outreach](references/cold-outreach.md). Keep sending separate from drafting and retain the source behind personalized claims.
