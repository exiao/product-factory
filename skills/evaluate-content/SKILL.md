# Evaluate content

Give an evidence-backed editorial judgment and the revisions that would most improve the requested piece. Review is read-only by default: quote problem passages and propose specific changes. Apply a rewrite when requested; evaluating a draft does not authorize publishing or changing an external document.

Read the complete supplied draft and relevant brief before judging it. For linked material, retrieve the actual article rather than judging a search snippet or generated summary; state any inaccessible sections. Use the intended reader, surface, purpose, and author's voice from context. If something material is missing, ask a focused question or label a reasonable provisional assumption.

## Six editorial lenses

Apply the lenses relevant to the piece; they are not mandatory scoring gates.

1. **Reader fit:** Who needs this, what brought them here, and what should they understand, feel, or do afterward? Point out competing audiences or missing context.
2. **Substance:** What does the reader learn or enjoy? Trace consequential claims to supplied evidence, distinguish opinion from fact, and identify unsupported leaps. An editorial review is not a completed fact-check unless sources were actually checked.
3. **Angle:** State the central argument or experience in one sentence. Identify specific insight, observation, or access that earns this version of the piece. Balance, neutrality, and narrow expertise can be appropriate; do not manufacture controversy or emotion.
4. **Structure and leanness:** Does the opening earn attention, does each section advance the piece, and does the payoff satisfy its promise? Name redundant passages and concrete cuts. Preserve context, uncertainty, caveats, and examples that carry meaning. Do not invent a cut quota.
5. **Voice and readability:** Judge against the intended author and surface. For Eric-authored publishable writing, consult the installed [$writer](../writer/SKILL.md) skill when voice evaluation is needed. For other authors use their brief or samples. Do not impose Eric's phrase bans, add invented first-person experience, or infer AI authorship from cadence or punctuation.
6. **Resonance and shareability:** Where relevant, identify a line, insight, or useful takeaway a reader might save or share, and explain why. Treat this as a hypothesis, not a prediction of reach. A reference article or niche explanation need not be broadly viral to succeed.

Keep precise odd words, useful repetition, long sentences, and uneven sections when they serve the author. Flag formulaic framing only when it weakens this piece; every style preference needs a concrete consequence.

For AI-led concepts or scripts, use [AI creative judgment](../content-factory/references/ai-creative-judgment.md) when relevant. Identify the actual laugh, learning, or chosen story payoff and whether it survives without novelty about AI. Distinguish clearly fictional emotion from fabricated lived experience. For ads, inspect whether the product belongs in the causal story rather than only the end card. Flag empty spectacle, unearned sentiment, and unsupported performance claims with the specific beat that fails. A script review does not establish that timing, acting, or sound works in the final media.

## Useful output

Lead with a short verdict relative to the brief. Then give the few highest-impact changes, each tied to a passage and a reason. Include what should survive revision when it helps prevent over-editing. Use `CUT: passage — reason` or `REVISE: passage → proposed alternative` when useful; propose no change if none earns its place.

Use qualitative labels such as strong, needs work, or weak only when comparison helps. If the user asks for scores, define the rubric and tie each score to evidence; do not use an arbitrary total as permission to publish. Match feedback length to the artifact: a headline may need only a few lines. Distinguish factual defects, missed brief requirements, and optional taste choices.

When rewriting, preserve facts, point of view, certainty, and genuine voice. Mark missing support or write around the gap; never manufacture statistics, testimonials, guarantees, or personal stories to make copy stronger. Recheck the revised piece against the original brief, not just the critique.
