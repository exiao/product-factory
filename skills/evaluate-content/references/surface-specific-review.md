# Surface-specific review

## Conversion copy

Use these as selective lenses rather than seven separate mandatory passes:

- **Clarity:** Resolve ambiguous subjects, pronouns, jargon, and missing context. Necessary technical terms may be clearer than vague simplification.
- **Voice:** Identify unintended shifts relative to the brand or author; a deliberate change in mood can serve the piece.
- **Consequence:** Connect features to a relevant reader outcome when that connection is supported. Do not invent a time saving or benefit to complete the sentence.
- **Proof:** Match a consequential claim to appropriate evidence. A testimonial is a reported experience, not proof of typical results; a guarantee is an offer term, not proof of performance.
- **Specificity:** Prefer real mechanisms, examples, quantities, and timeframes over vague praise. Missing detail is a gap to flag, not permission to substitute a plausible number.
- **Emotion:** Check whether the emotional register fits the reader's situation. Plain informational copy may be right; do not heighten fear or add a fictional anecdote by default.
- **Decision friction:** Make the action and its consequences understandable. Verify any proposed free trial, refund, cancellation, privacy, or pricing claim against actual terms. Do not promise zero risk or remove material qualifications.

Return the most consequential revisions, not one finding per lens. Copy quality alone does not establish conversion lift; name a test or evidence gap when performance is the question.

## Short-form scripts and hooks

Evaluate the opening promise, who it is for, why the next beat follows, and whether the payoff delivers. A feature-led hook or narrow audience can work when it matches the brief. Do not require a universal audience, suspense, or an answer withheld until the final frame.

For silent-feed distribution, inspect whether visuals and captions communicate the necessary context without sound. Use the supplied distribution requirements; do not apply a fixed 15-second test to every duration or audio-led film. If only a script is available, label visual, pacing, and sound observations as proposed checks rather than completed viewing.

Look for a broken promise between hook and body, disconnected shots, unreadable text at intended display size, and explanation that arrives before its context. Recommend a specific replacement or reorder and explain the likely benefit without guaranteeing views or retention. File integrity and audio/render checks belong with video editing; this review judges the content actually available.
