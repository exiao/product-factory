# Company thesis review

Use for founder essays, category arguments, and related company posts when strategic substance matters beyond sentence quality. Judge the argument on its evidence, not on whether it makes the company seem inevitable.

- **Thesis:** Can the central claim be stated precisely? Do the sections support it, and do material counterexamples or assumptions change its reach?
- **First product and user:** Is there a concrete entry point and person served beneath the platform ambition? Do not force product promotion into an essay whose purpose is different.
- **Buyer perspective:** What would the intended reader need to believe, what evidence would satisfy them, and which objections remain?
- **Workflow:** Replace vague claims with an actual sequence of inputs, decisions, outputs, and outcomes where supplied evidence supports it.
- **Trust:** For claims that depend on reliability or accountability, examine verification, auditability, human responsibility, and relevant buyer requirements. Do not invent compliance status or demand a generic compliance section for every essay.
- **Category boundaries:** Clarify likely confusion with alternatives when it affects comprehension; a formulaic “not X, but Y” section is unnecessary.
- **Proof:** Separate production outcomes, experiments, anecdotes, and aspirations. Check whether examples support the scale and certainty of the thesis.
- **Forecasts:** Identify assumptions, horizon, and what would disprove the prediction. Distinguish a sourced forecast from the author's stated bet.

Across a sequence of posts, identify contradictions, repeated setup, and missing connective reasoning. Cite the actual passages. Recommend the smallest additions or cuts that close the argument; do not supply fabricated proof or turn the review into a new positioning project unless asked.
