## Discovery and scope: YC-style

Use the questions the existing evidence has not answered:

- Who specifically needs this, and in what situation?
- What do they do today, and what is unsatisfactory about it?
- Who has the strongest need or willingness to act, and what evidence supports that?
- What is the smallest version that delivers the actual value?
- What observation challenges the usual assumption about this problem?
- Could this grow into a product or is it better as a feature, service, or internal tool?

Answer from supplied research or code before asking the user. Do not assume the literal request is wrong or force a reframe. Turn material assumptions into falsifiable premises and state the evidence that would overturn them. Preserve accepted decisions instead of repeatedly seeking approval.

Match scope behavior to the request:

| Intent | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Hold scope | Apply rigor to the agreed work without introducing expansion. |
| Reduce | Find the smallest coherent outcome and defer the rest. |
| Explore selectively | Compare a few worthwhile extensions to the existing baseline. |
| Expand | Explore an ambitious experience, then find a feasible first step. |

Infer an already clear intent instead of making the user select a mode. For an implementation request or locked plan, hold scope. Ambitious or “10-star” thinking applies when exploration is requested; it is not a reason to replace an ordinary ticket with a larger product. A proposed expansion remains optional until selected or its choice has been delegated.

When alternatives matter, compare a few approaches by what ships, what is learned, dependencies, effort uncertainty, and what is deferred. Estimate from actual work and constraints; do not invent human-versus-AI speedups. Recommend one and explain the tradeoff.

Record an agreed plan only when the task needs a durable handoff, using the project's existing location and conventions. Include problem, premises, selected scope, first increment, evidence plan, and open questions. Avoid mandatory personal reflections, duplicate vision documents, or a new approval ceremony.
