# Idea validation

## Still-image generation

For generated or edited still images, use [$image-generation-guide](../image-generation-guide/SKILL.md): Codex built-in image generation, verified files saved in the local project. This applies to reference sheets and intermediate assets as well as final images. Keep this skill's creative requirements; do not automatically use provider CLIs, external generation plugins, or remote rendering services. An explicit user request for another provider takes precedence.

Use Eric's Ad Test to make an idea concrete before adding analysis. Read the idea list and constraints. Preserve every named idea and distinguish acquisition promise, actual product capability, and retention value. Default to concise comparisons rather than numerical scorecards; use scores only when requested, with explicit assumptions.

For each idea write:

- **Buyer and placement:** a specific audience and a plausible way to reach it.
- **Pitch:** one short customer-facing line in the buyer's language.
- **Price:** the existing price or an explicitly labeled proposed price, including billing period or unit. Do not present a guessed price as validated willingness to pay.
- **Read:** why the pitch seems compelling or where it breaks, plus the assumption most worth testing.

Group ideas by clarity: a pitch that stands on its own, a pitch that depends on resolving one major objection, or an unclear offer/distribution path. These are triage judgments, not market verdicts. A weak ad can reveal the wrong framing, a free substitute, missing proof, or a sales motion that needs a demo or conversation. Enterprise sales and community products are not invalid just because a social ad is a poor fit.

Assess the founder's practical advantage: access to a buyer, domain knowledge, trusted data, workflow integration, cost to deliver, and ability to support the product. Vertical niches and existing assets may make an idea feasible, but company size alone does not determine who can compete. Treat claims about durable moats or rapidly depreciating infrastructure as hypotheses to examine against the actual product.

If asked about existing app opportunities, use dated, available research sources and verify rankings, reviews, pricing, and the actual differentiated product experience. Prometheus may help when available. Viral hooks and top-grossing charts can identify leads; they do not establish profitability, replicability, or a guaranteed distribution advantage. Adapt legitimate product patterns and customer needs without copying protected assets or inventing endorsements.

For first-customer, manual-service, or bootstrap questions, read the Sahil-inspired section of [references/office-hours.md](references/office-hours.md). For problem discovery, scope reduction, or explicitly requested ambitious exploration, read its YC-style section. Reuse the current brief; these lenses do not add a mandatory interview or expansion phase. Use [$pricing](../pricing/SKILL.md) for detailed packaging decisions and [$growth](../growth/SKILL.md) for activation, retention, and experiments when relevant.

For a build/no-build proposal, make the decision useful without imposing a fixed template. Reuse the stated audience and pitch, then identify the evidence that the problem exists, the current workaround, why it merits attention now, the smallest useful change or demand test, and what competing work it would displace. Separate observed demand from founder intuition or a persuasive mock ad. If evidence is missing, name the gap rather than inventing customer signals.

Give the test an observable outcome and a relevant observation window. Define what result would support building, revising the idea, or deferring it; use an existing baseline or a clearly labeled proposed threshold rather than presenting a guessed target as validated. End a proposal with a recommendation and the main uncertainty that could reverse it. Quick idea comparisons need only the distinctions that change the ranking, not a full proposal for every option. Reuse the current brief and acceptance criteria rather than creating a parallel document or mandatory interview.

Finish with the strongest candidates and the smallest credible test that could change the decision. Read [references/ad-test-and-evidence.md](references/ad-test-and-evidence.md) for visual comparisons and demand evidence. When distribution choice, adoption, or payment is part of the decision, read [references/distribution-fit-and-demand-tests.md](references/distribution-fit-and-demand-tests.md) to diagnose buying readiness and switching costs, examine why now, compare channel fit, and set an observable demand test. Use [$positioning-angles](../positioning-angles/SKILL.md) if available for deeper framing, or handle that narrow question directly; its absence must not block the task.

Creating a comparison or mock ad does not authorize launching ads, contacting buyers, collecting payments, publishing a landing page, or building the product. Continue those steps when included in the user's existing authorization, without asking again for the same action.
