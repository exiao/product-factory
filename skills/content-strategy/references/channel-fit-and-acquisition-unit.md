# Channel fit and acquisition units

Use this reference when a content strategy must choose an initial channel or explain how content could acquire attention. A channel is a working choice under current evidence and capacity, not a permanent product constraint.

## Choose the initial channel

Compare likely channels against the strategy's outcome:

- audience presence and intent;
- fit between the product truth and the channel's native format;
- the team's ability to make, learn from, and sustain the work;
- reach, production cost, and any authorized media budget;
- the quality of the measurement path to the next action.

Choose one primary learning surface when focus helps, while keeping a secondary reuse path only when it does not dilute the test. Revisit the choice when audience or downstream evidence changes.

## Specify the repeatable unit

For each prioritized series or batch, define:

- the audience situation and attention hook;
- the asset format, length or scope, and production effort;
- the proof, demonstration, or useful takeaway;
- the product connection and next action;
- the owner, feasible cadence, and success measure.

Prefer a small set of units that can produce learning across several angles. A high-view format is a hypothesis until it produces the outcome the strategy needs. A content-first test can be useful when the content itself demonstrates the proposed value, but it is not a prerequisite for every product or channel.
