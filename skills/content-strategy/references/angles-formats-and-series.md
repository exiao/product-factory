# Angles, formats, and series

Match the message to what this audience already understands. Problem-unaware audiences may need a recognizable situation; problem-aware audiences need a useful way forward; solution-aware readers need differentiation; product-aware readers need proof and objections addressed; ready buyers need an accurate offer. Treat these as hypotheses about the actual audience, not channel-wide labels.

Generate distinct angles from real sources: a human situation, a demonstrated result, a useful process, a specific misconception, an honest comparison, or a recurring question. Evaluate relevance, proof, product connection, production cost, and freshness. Use qualitative judgments with reasons instead of invented probabilities that an AI would generate an idea or that it will win.

Formats serve different jobs. Demonstrations show mechanism; tutorials teach; comparisons support decisions; stories can create identification; case studies show supported outcomes; reactions need original analysis. Label speculative case studies and fictional scenarios. A reference's longevity or high view count does not establish sales performance.

For short visual demos, test whether the essential action is understandable without sound when muted viewing matters. Add captions and accessible context as needed. Do not force every topic into 15 seconds or assume sound-off comprehension proves conversion.

A series needs a recognizable promise, repeatable structure, episode scope, and sustainable production method. Reuse the approved character configuration when one exists. Decide whether episodes stand alone or form an arc; generate the number of ideas needed for the actual batch. Balance timely pieces with evergreen material. Fit scripts to current platform requirements and measured speech, not historical duration limits.

Each episode brief should name the hook, useful payoff, source/proof, visual method, and intended next step. Choose a small feasible batch; do not automatically create 20 episodes or use a predictor's score as audience evidence.
