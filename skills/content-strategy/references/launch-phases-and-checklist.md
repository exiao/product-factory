# Launch phases and checklist

Use this reference for a product, feature, beta, or release campaign. Adapt the phases to the actual readiness and audience; a small update may need only a focused announcement and follow-up.

## Phases

1. **Readiness:** verify the promise, audience, product truth, destination, instrumentation, support path, and the evidence needed to make a credible claim.
2. **Early learning:** put the smallest useful version in front of an appropriate set of users or readers, collect qualitative friction and observable behavior, and fix the gaps that would undermine the promise.
3. **Early access or beta:** give interested people a clear reason to join, explain the current limits and access terms, and use the resulting questions and usage to sharpen the message and onboarding.
4. **Launch moment:** publish the clearest announcement through the authorized owned, platform, or partner surfaces. Make the next action obvious and keep the content useful even when a reader is not ready to convert.
5. **Follow-through:** review reach, qualified engagement, activation, retention, leads, or purchases according to the goal; gather feedback; document what changed; and choose the next update or experiment.

Owned, platform, and partner channels are planning categories, not a requirement to use every channel. Product Hunt, waitlists, influencer outreach, email, and paid promotion are optional tactics that require fit and explicit scope.

## Planning checklist

Before launch, confirm the audience and promise, the offer or access terms, the landing or in-product destination, the launch assets, ownership, tracking, and a review date. During launch, confirm the authorized surfaces, the response owner, the proof and caveats included in the message, and the signal being watched. After launch, compare results with a relevant baseline, inspect the full funnel, record evidence gaps, and define the next decision.

Do not invent testimonials, urgency, adoption, or performance claims. A launch plan does not itself authorize publishing, outreach, paid spend, or recurring monitoring.
